# Drawfree

Storyboards you drag, not draw. A browser tool for building storyboard frames from full-body characters in the Open Peeps style. Users swap hair, faces, outfits and shoes, flip a character to face left or right, rotate it, and pose arms and legs by dragging hands and feet. No drawing skill needed.

Stack: Vite, React 18, TypeScript (strict), Zustand. Static site on Cloudflare Workers. No accounts, free forever, no watermark.

Hard rule: nothing in `src/parts`, `src/rig` or `src/scenes` may import React or touch the DOM. They take data in and return SVG strings out. `tools/check.js` enforces it. Biome bans `any`.

### Character model

A character is plain data from `newCharacter()`: part ids (`body`, `hair`, `face`, `top`, `bottom`, `shoes`, `glasses`, `facial`), colours (`skin`, `hairC`, `topC`, `botC`, `shoeC`, `hatC`), placement (`x`, `y`, `s` scale, `r` rotation in degrees, `flip`) and `pose`. Items are `kind: 'char'` or `kind: 'prop'` (with `prop: <id>`). The SVG is regenerated from this data on every change. Never store SVG in state. The in-memory shape is the project file shape (spec data model).

### Coordinate space and rig

- Local space: pelvis near (0,0), figure faces +x, ground line `GROUND` (189, in `rig/geometry.ts`), y grows down. The head art is anchored at (0,-212) in Open Peeps body space: the importer maps Open Peeps head space so the point between the eyes (head-local 309,296) lands on (13.5,-214) at scale 0.144. Every Open Peeps body, the puppet included, is drawn in its art's space moved down `GROUND - box[3]` onto the ground line, with the head inside the same move.
- Left-facing is `scale(-1 1)` on the `.body` group. Position, rotation and scale sit on the outer `.item` group.
- Each limb is two bones. `pose` holds `[upperAngle, lowerAngle, bendSign]` per limb (`aL` near arm, `aR` far arm, `lL` near leg, `lR` far leg). Angles are degrees, 0 is straight down, positive swings toward +x; the lower angle is relative to the upper bone.
- `limbsOf(c)` gives each limb's root, bone lengths and rest angles for the character's garments (arms from the top's family, legs from the bottom's). `solveLimb(c, limb, target, sign)` is IK plus the joint limits (`limit`: elbows and knees fold at most 150/145 degrees either way, thighs swing -80 to 125); the store and the drag handler use it, and `rig(c)` applies the limits too. Presets in `POSES` are hand and foot targets in local space for the limbs they move (the rest stay as drawn; `idle` is the art's own pose, `restPose`).

### The posable figure: a cutout puppet

The founder rejected the hand-drawn figure (PR 13): the posable figure is real Open Peeps art cut into pieces. A **family** (`parts/cutout.ts`) is a group of Open Peeps standing bodies drawn on the same geometry (stand-shirt-3 and stand-shirt-4 differ only in which garments are dark); a garment (`parts/garments.ts`) is one of those bodies' tops, bottoms or shoes.

- **Pieces**: torso (with neck), pelvis (trousers above the hips), upper and lower arm per side (the hand rides on the forearm), thigh, shin and foot per side. Each piece is a `<use>` of the garment's art, defined once per character in `<defs>`, through its own clip shape (a `<mask>`, see Gotchas), with a matrix that turns it about its joint (`rig/puppet.ts`). Torso and arms come from the top's art, pelvis and legs from the bottom's, feet from the shoes'. At rest every matrix is a pure translation, so the puppet is the art exactly (tested).
- **Clip shapes are traced, not drawn** (`tools/cutout.ts`, output `parts/peeps/cutout.gen.ts`, a lazy chunk loaded with the body art). The art is rasterised crisp in layer order, fill areas flood-filled and split by hand-placed cut lines (`CUTS`: where a sleeve runs into the shoulder, or the trousers run from the waist to the hem), each area goes to the nearest bone and then by colour kind (shoes to feet, trouser fill to the pelvis), and each piece takes the ink bordering its fills. Lines between side-by-side pieces go to both, so either keeps an outline when they part; `front` pairs give a line to the piece on top only (the hand over the trousers, the shirt hem). `own` fixes the odd area the nearest bone gets wrong. Check with `review`, place joints with `measure` (the art's span across each limb; hand-reading coordinates off zoomed screenshots was off by 2 to 3 units).
- **Hiding the cuts**: joint caps (`@` in `ORDER`) are disks of the garment colour with an ink ring at shoulders, elbows, hips and knees, as wide as the limb and clipped to what the later pieces cover at rest, so they never show as drawn and fill the wedge a bent joint opens. Underlays (`under`) are the torso or pelvis as it would be without the limb in front (so a moved hand leaves no hole) or the part of a limb the torso hides (the far upper arm's band); their ink edge `e` is open and sits just inside the line it continues. Seams (`seams`) are ink along cuts with no line in the art (the near arm's armpit), fading in over the first 12 degrees of turn.
- **Draw order** (`ORDER`): far leg, near leg (each cap, shin, foot, thigh), pelvis, far upper arm (behind the torso, as drawn), torso, near arm, head, far forearm (in front, so crossed arms and hands on hips read).
- **Measuring**: browsers measure a masked element by its whole art (`getBBox` and `getBoundingClientRect` both, checked in Chromium), so pieces are `.cut` and the puppet adds a `.bounds` path (each piece's convex hull, placed); `editor/measure.ts` measures a figure by everything but `.cut`. Ids in the puppet's defs are prefixed `pp-<item id>`, so items on one page never clash.
- **Comparison**: `docs/cutout/*.png` (regenerate with `compare`) put the puppet next to real Open Peeps poses: rest, arms raised, crossed, walking, sitting, and elbows and knees bent step by step. Look at them after any change to cuts, joints, caps or underlays.
- **Adding a family**: add it to `FAMILIES` (base body, joints from `measure`, underlays, seams) and to `CUTS` in the tool (cut lines, `front`, `own`, `across`), run the tool, `review` and `compare`, then add its garments to `parts/garments.ts`.

### Open Peeps parts (milestone 6)

Hairstyles (each includes the head), expressions, facial hair and glasses come from Open Peeps (CC0, `LICENSES.md`). `tools/import-peeps.ts` reads the pack from `assets/open-peeps/` (gitignored), maps every path into rig space, rounds to 0.1 and writes compact relative path data to `src/parts/peeps/*.gen.ts`. `src/parts/heads.ts` names them, tags them and sets picker order; it throws if an import adds or drops a part without a name. `peepSVG(art, {skin, hair, hat})` draws them.

- The pack is black and white: one white silhouette under black ink. Each layer has a kind: `s` skin, `h` hair colour, `a` hat colour, `w` white, `i` ink, or a fixed colour. White in a head is skin, in facial hair it is light hair, in glasses it is white.
- Light hair, hats and headbands are white in the pack too, so the importer splits the white into regions (`tools/peeps-regions.ts`: rasterise with resvg, flood fill, trace the chosen regions back to polygons grown 1.5 units so they hide under the ink). `REGIONS` in the importer lists which region of which head is hair or hat, by a seed point; `cuts` close ink gaps where hair strokes stop short of the face. `review` draws a numbered map with a 25-unit grid. The importer refuses a seed that lands in the face.
- Faces: every hole the ink fully encloses (eye whites, teeth) is filled white, or it would show the skin colour.
- Dark hair and dark garments are solid ink in the pack. The importer finds the solid parts of the ink (`thickInk`: erode by `ERODE` so strokes vanish, grow back by `REGROW` so an ink ring stays as the outline) and draws them over the ink in the hair colour (every head and beard; the hijab and wide-brim hat in the hat colour), the top colour (waist-up bodies, except `BUST_KEEP_INK`), or, for full bodies, by `FAMILIES`: siblings with the same geometry whose light garment is labelled say which solid ink is top, trousers or shoes. Cycling and Legs crossed have no light sibling, so their trousers stay black. `HAIRC` starts with black (the default). Bear (a shaggy beard-and-hair mass with no drawn face edge) stays skin-coloured.
- Hats are hairstyles (Beanie, Wide-brim hat, Turban, Hijab), coloured by `hatC`. There is no hat slot and no neckwear any more (Open Peeps has none). Project files are version 3 (see Project files); `MIGRATIONS[1]` maps the old head ids (crop, curly, grin, round, beard, beanie...) to the Peeps ones, and the legacy autosave reader does the same.

### Bodies

`body` is `'rig'` (the posable figure, a cutout puppet dressed by the top, bottom and shoe garments) or an Open Peeps body: 23 standing (`stand-*`), 11 sitting (`sit-*`, the bike and wheelchair included) and 30 waist up (`bust-*`). `parts/bodies.ts` names and tags them; `isPosable(c)` says which kind a character has.

- **Art loads on demand.** The bodies are about 176 KB gzipped, a separate chunk: `loadBodyArt()` fetches it (main.tsx calls `loadArt` at start, every export awaits it), `bodyArt(id)` returns it once in. Until then a character shows its head only. The store's `art` counter bumps on arrival; `ItemView`, the frame strip, the Overlay and the picker thumbnails (`bodyArtVersion()` in the cache key) redraw on it. `tools/check.js` fails the build if the art lands in the first-load bundle. Tests load it in `src/test/setup.ts`.
- **Placement.** The importer writes each body's bounds (`bodies.box.gen.ts`, in the main bundle). `charSVG` moves the body and its head down by `GROUND - box[3]` so every body stands on the posable figure's soles (`GROUND = 189`); a waist-up body rests its bottom edge there and has no shadow. `charBounds` sizes the click and focus area from the same bounds.
- **Colour.** Skin, top, bottom and shoe regions of every body were labelled by hand in `REGIONS` (tools/import-peeps.ts) and checked on a coloured contact sheet. White soles, laces, prosthetic legs, the bike and the wheelchair stay white. Black garments are ink, as in Open Peeps.
- **Posing.** Pose handles and the Pose button exist only for the posable figure. Picking a preset pose switches a character to it; picking an Open Peeps body turns pose mode off. New characters get an Open Peeps body (`NEW_BODY`); the seed has one of each; files without a `body` load as the posable figure.
- **Picker.** The Body tab holds the posable figure's preset poses, then the Standing, Sitting and Waist-up bodies. Body thumbnails are framed one by one (`viewOf` in `ui/sections.ts`); every other thumbnail is drawn on the posable figure, whose position its view is framed for. On an Open Peeps body the Tops, Bottoms and Shoes tabs say that the parts dress the posable figure and the colours recolour the body.

### Adding a part

Add an entry to the right list (for example `PROPS` in `src/parts/props.ts`) with `id`, `name`, `tags: tags('space separated words')` and what its type in `types.ts` asks for, using `INK` outlines (stroke `W`, 2.3, matched to the Open Peeps ink). Garments for the posable figure are Open Peeps art: a garment names its family and the body art it comes from (`parts/garments.ts`); a new family needs the cutout steps above. A new category also needs a section in `src/ui/sections.ts` (and a slot in `PART_LISTS` if it is a character slot). Thumbnails, search and the picker UI follow from that. Run `npm test` (it draws every part with every palette colour and checks the SVG parses) and `npm run sheet`.

## State and editor behaviour to preserve

- **Immutable state.** Every action builds new frames and items; never edit in place. `React.memo` on `ItemView` and cheap undo both depend on it. Past snapshots are plain references.
- **History.** `snap()` pushes one undo step. Call it once before a mutation. For drags it runs only after the pointer has moved 3px (move) or at all (other drags), so a click never makes an undo step. Typed text (note, project name) makes no step per keystroke; a bubble's text snaps once on focus.
- **Drags.** `editor/interactions.ts` keeps the drag session in a ref with `pointermove`/`pointerup` listeners on `window`. Never attach drag state to elements: the stage re-renders on every change.
- **Joint dragging** uses the inverse of the `.body` group's `getScreenCTM()`, so joints land on the pointer after flip, rotation and scale.
- The selection box turns with the item (see roadmap item 5). The Overlay re-measures in `useLayoutEffect` after every render.
- Thumbnails (picker and frame strip) are `<img>` data URLs (`svgImgSrc`), not inline SVG: far fewer DOM nodes, and their text stays out of the accessibility tree. The frame strip's order and current ring follow the live list; only the thumbnail art is deferred.
- The rotate handle sits above the (turned) selection box, else below it if that stays inside the stage, else beside it. (On phones the toolbar sits under the stage, so a handle below the box used to be hidden behind it.)
- Pose handles and hit areas exist only in the editor. `itemSVG` (export, thumbnails) emits no editor chrome. While Pose is on, the scale corners are hidden (they sat on raised hands).
- The ground shadow shows only when rotation is under 8 degrees.
- The picker and frame strip use `useDeferredValue`, so they catch up when a drag settles instead of re-rendering per pointer move. Thumbnails are cached in `ui/thumbs.ts` by section, part and the character's look.
- Dialogs use `ui/Dialog.tsx` (focus moves in, to the element marked `data-autofocus` or the first control; Tab is trapped, Esc and backdrop close, focus returns). Never use `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt`: use the toast and dialogs.
- **Markup from strings.** Art is SVG strings injected with `svgMarkup()` (`ui/markup.ts`, the only place `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` is used). Only pass strings built by the pure part, rig and scene functions, which escape user text with `esc()`. Never pass user input, file contents or anything not drawn by our own code.
- Autosave (`state/persist.ts`): key `drawfree.v1`, debounced 500 ms, flushed on `pagehide` and when hidden, never throws, warns once if storage is blocked. It reads the old prototype shape (`type`/`kind` items) and the older `inkframe.v1` key.

## Export

- **Frame**: SVG, or PNG at 1x/2x/3x, optional transparent background (`export/frame.ts`, `svg.ts`, `png.ts`).
- **Board PNG strip** (`board.ts`): every frame drawn onto one canvas in a grid (1 to 4 columns, 480/720/960 px frames) with numbered captions and optional notes. The canvas text uses Figtree, so any script works. `layout.ts` shrinks the frames when the image would pass the browser's canvas limits (16 million pixels, the Safari cap) and throws if even tiny frames cannot fit.
- **Board PDF** (`pdf.ts`): A4 or Letter, landscape or portrait, one frame per page or a 2 or 3 per row contact sheet. Frames are PNG images (2x), notes are real text. pdf-lib's built-in Helvetica covers Latin-1 only, so other characters (emoji, CJK, Arabic) become `?` and the toast says how many. Fixing that needs an embedded font (`@pdf-lib/fontkit` plus a font file, also loaded on demand).
- **pdf-lib is a dynamic import** and a separate 172 KB chunk. `tools/check.js` fails the build if it ever lands in the first-load bundle. Keep `import('pdf-lib')` inside `buildPdf`.
- All layout math is pure (`export/layout.ts`) and tested in Node. `buildPdf` takes the frame renderer as an argument, so its tests run real pdf-lib without a canvas. The canvas parts (`board.ts`, `png.ts`) are only checked in a real browser.
- The export dialog remembers its choices in localStorage (`drawfree.export`), guarded for blocked storage.

## Frames

Add, duplicate, delete, reorder, and a note per frame. Reordering (`moveFrame`) works three ways: drag a thumbnail (mouse and pen; `FrameStrip` keeps the session in a ref with window listeners, like the stage), the Move earlier and Move later buttons, and Alt+Arrow on a focused thumbnail. Touch drags are left to the strip to scroll, so touch users use the buttons. A move is one undo step, the current frame stays current, and a toast announces it. The strip compares the current frame by id, not by index, because the thumbnails are a deferred value.

## Project files

`export/project.ts` is the only door for data from outside (opened files, localStorage): `parseProject`, `parseProjectObject`, `cleanFrames`. It repairs rather than rejects: unknown part ids fall back to the section's first part, bad colours to defaults, numbers are clamped, XML-illegal characters stripped, unknown props dropped (counted, shown in a toast). Files from a newer version are refused. To change the format: bump `PROJECT_VERSION` and add `MIGRATIONS[oldVersion]`. Version 3 (`MIGRATIONS[2]`) maps the hand-drawn tops, bottoms and shoes (tee, hoodie, jeans, skirt, sneaker, boot...) to the Open Peeps garments; poses are bone angles either way and carry over, and the legacy autosave reader migrates too. New character fields go in `PART_LISTS`/`COLOR_SLOTS` handling in `cleanItem`. File names: `<project-name>-frame-<n>.<ext>`, `<project-name>.drawfree.json`.

5. **Editor polish (milestone 5): DONE.** Keyboard routes, Playwright and axe, screen-pixel touch handles, zoom and pan, the rotated selection box and multi-select are all in. How each works, for whoever touches it next:
   - **Playwright and axe: DONE** (`e2e/`, `playwright.config.ts`, a CI job). About 130 tests across Chromium, WebKit, a Pixel 7 viewport and an iPad (WebKit): drag move, scale, rotate, hand-handle IK drag, bend flip, keyboard-only editing (Tab reaches items, focus selects), save and reopen a project, autosave, real PNG/SVG/PDF/strip downloads (checks file signatures and PNG size), frame reordering by button, key and drag, plus axe on the editor, selection, pose mode, every picker tab, search, the export dialog (all three scopes) and the frame strip. When zoom lands, re-measure on zoom changes too (the observer only sees size changes).
   - **Zoom and pan: DONE.** `editor/view.ts` (pure maths, tested in Node) holds the view as a zoom (1 to 5) and a centre; `Stage` sets its `viewBox` from `store.view`, which is not in undo history and not saved, and resets when a project is opened. Ctrl/Cmd+wheel and a trackpad pinch zoom about the pointer (native non-passive wheel listener in `Editor.tsx`); the plain wheel pans once zoomed in; Space+drag and the middle button pan; two fingers pinch (`interactions.ts`, `pinchOf`); `0` resets; with nothing selected `+`/`-` zoom and arrows pan (with an item selected they edit the item); `ZoomControls` gives on-screen buttons. The Overlay and `useStagePx` re-measure after every render, so the selection box and handle sizes follow the zoom. Caveats: Space on a focused button also presses it (blur first); the two-finger pinch is only tested with synthetic pointer events in jsdom, not on a real touch device.
   - **Rotated selection box: DONE.** `Overlay` measures the `.figure` and `.handles` groups inside `.body` (not the ground shadow, which is wider than the character and disappears past 8 degrees of turn), unions their `getBBox()`es, maps the four corners through `body.getScreenCTM()` and `box.ts` turns that into a centre, size, angle and bounds. The box is a div with `transform: rotate()`; the corners and the rotate handle are its children, so they ride along. The handle goes above the box in its own turned frame, else below, else beside (`handleSide`). The toolbar is placed from the axis-aligned bounds. Item markup is now `.body > .shadow + .figure (+ .handles)`.
   - **Multi-select: DONE.** The store keeps `sel` (the primary item: the last one picked, which pose mode and the box handles work on) and `extra` (the others, never containing `sel`); `selectedIds`/`selectedItems` give all of them. Shift-click toggles an item (`toggleSelect`); a drag on empty stage draws a marquee (`store.marquee`, drawn by `Overlay`) that selects every item whose figure it touches, and Shift adds to the selection; Ctrl/Cmd+A selects all. Dragging any selected item moves the whole group (`patchItems`, one undo step). Delete, duplicate (copies become the selection), bring to front (keeps relative order), flip (skips speech bubbles), nudge, scale, rotate and reset rotation apply to every selected item, each as one undo step; the picker edits every selected character. A group has one upright dashed box and the toolbar says \"N items selected\"; it has no scale corners, no rotate handle and no Pose button (those stay single-item). Undo and changing frame prune the selection.
   - Direct joint dragging stays pointer-only; that gap is documented in the spec.

**Keyboard**: Tab reaches each item on the stage (the transparent hit area is the focus stop and carries the name, e.g. "Character 1" or "Speech bubble: <text>"); focusing selects it, Enter or Space selects too. **Shortcuts (all in `editor/useKeyboard.ts`)**: arrows nudge 1 px, Shift+arrows 10 px; `+`/`-` scale; `[` and `]` rotate 5 degrees (Shift 15); `F` flips; Delete or Backspace removes; Ctrl/Cmd+D duplicates; Alt+`]` brings to front; Esc deselects (or closes a dialog); Ctrl/Cmd+Z undo, Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z redo; Ctrl/Cmd+S save, Ctrl/Cmd+O open; `0` resets zoom; Ctrl/Cmd+wheel zoom, Space+drag pan (with nothing selected: `+`/`-` zoom, arrows pan); `/` focuses search; arrows in the picker move between thumbnails; Alt+arrows on a frame thumbnail reorder frames.

## Gotchas

- Aspect ratio: v1 is 16:9 only, by the founder's decision. The `aspect` field stays in the project file and is preserved, but nothing reads it.
- Scene line colour switches by background luminance. Character outlines are always near-black, so dark backgrounds make them hard to see. Keep backgrounds light unless outlines become themeable.
- PNG export draws the SVG through an `<img>`, which cannot use the page's web fonts, so speech-bubble text falls back to the system font in PNGs until the fonts are self-hosted and embedded. The root `<svg>` needs explicit `width`/`height` for Safari and Firefox. Not yet tested in Safari or Firefox.
- The standalone page loads Figtree and Bricolage Grotesque from Google Fonts.
- In Playwright, `boundingBox()` of an SVG element includes its stroke width but `getBoundingClientRect()` (what the app measures) does not; when comparing against the selection box, measure with `getBoundingClientRect` in `page.evaluate`.
- jsdom stubs (`src/test/setup.ts`) fake `getScreenCTM`, `DOMPoint` and layout, so tests prove the logic, not the geometry. Geometry (drag lands on the pointer, selection box fits) was checked in a real browser.
- The old Claude artifact build (`dist/artifact.html`) is gone with the vanilla build. Downloads are blocked inside Claude's artifact viewer; Copy SVG (with its manual-copy dialog) is the fallback.
- TypeScript 7 rejects some regex literals the older compiler accepted; `XML_ILLEGAL` is built with `new RegExp(string)` for that reason.
- The puppet's pieces use `<mask>`, not `clip-path`: Chrome clips each path of the art as it draws it, so along a cut a dark garment (colour laid over solid ink, as Open Peeps draws dark clothes) showed its ink as a grey hairline at 1x. A mask applies to the finished piece. resvg showed nothing, so check cut changes in a real browser at 1x.
- Linting is Biome, not ESLint: `typescript-eslint` does not support TypeScript 7 yet (peer range stops below 6.1). Revisit if that changes; do not force the install.
